## Value Propositions (The Big 5)

### 1. Hand-Coded, No WordPress Bloat
**What it means:** Every site is built from scratch with clean HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. No WordPress, no themes, no page builders, no plugins.

**Why it matters:** Hand-coded sites load 3-5x faster than WordPress sites. Faster sites rank higher on Google, have lower bounce rates, and convert more visitors. No plugin conflicts, no security vulnerabilities, no forced updates that break things.

**How to say it:**
- "Your site is built from scratch — no WordPress, no templates, no bloat. Just clean code that loads fast and ranks well."
- "While most freelancers buy a $60 theme and call it custom, I hand-code every line. That's why my sites score 90+ on Google's speed test."

### 2. Finance Background = ROI Thinking
**What it means:** Notre Dame finance degree. Every design decision is made through the lens of return on investment, not just aesthetics.

**Why it matters:** Most web designers think about how a site looks. Andrew thinks about how a site performs. Every element has a purpose: drive calls, bookings, inquiries. Business owners speak ROI — and so does Andrew.

**How to say it:**
- "I come from finance, not art school. Every design decision I make is about converting visitors into customers."
- "I don't just make your site look good — I make it work. My finance background means I think about ROI on every pixel."

### 3. Free Audit = Proof Before Purchase
**What it means:** Any business can get a comprehensive, no-strings-attached website audit before spending a dime.

**Why it matters:** Eliminates risk for the client. They see exactly what's wrong with their current site, with data and screenshots, before committing. Builds trust, demonstrates expertise, and creates urgency.

**How to say it:**
- "I'll audit your website for free — show you exactly what's holding you back, with real data. No strings attached."
- "Before you spend a dollar, let me show you what Google thinks of your current site. The results might surprise you."

### 4. Full Ownership, No Lock-In
**What it means:** Client owns 100% of the code, design, and content. No monthly platform fees. No proprietary CMS. They can take it anywhere.

**Why it matters:** Wix and Squarespace charge monthly fees forever, and if you leave, you lose your site. WordPress locks you into their ecosystem. With a hand-coded site, the client owns everything and pays no recurring platform costs.

**How to say it:**
- "You own your site completely — the code, the design, everything. No monthly fees, no lock-in, no platform holding your site hostage."
- "When we're done, you own it all. Unlike Wix or WordPress, there's no subscription to keep your own site online."

### 5. Fast Delivery (AI-Augmented Workflow)
**What it means:** Sites are delivered in 1-2 weeks vs. the industry standard of 4-8 weeks. Made possible by an AI-augmented workflow using Claude.

**Why it matters:** Time is money. Local business owners don't want to wait 2 months for a website. Faster delivery means they start generating leads sooner.

**How to say it:**
- "I deliver in 1-2 weeks what most agencies take 2 months to build. Same quality, fraction of the time."
- "Your new site can be live in as little as one week. No endless timelines, no scope creep, no delays."

**Note on AI messaging:** Lead with the speed, not the tool. Most clients don't care how the work gets done — they care about the result. Don't emphasize AI unless the client is tech-savvy and asks about the process. Then position it as: "I use cutting-edge AI tools to accelerate the process while maintaining hand-crafted quality."

## Voice & Tone Guide

### Voice (Consistent Always)
- **Direct:** Say what you mean. No fluff, no filler, no corporate speak.
- **Data-driven:** Back up claims with numbers. Speed scores, conversion rates, timelines, dollar amounts.
- **Business-minded:** Speak the language of ROI, revenue, customers, growth. Not the language of "pixels" and "design systems."
- **Confident but not arrogant:** Know your worth, state your value, never oversell.
- **Accessible:** No tech jargon with clients. Translate everything into business outcomes.

### Tone (Adjusts by Context)
- **Cold email:** Professional, concise, value-first. Not salesy.
- **Discovery call:** Warm, curious, consultative. Mostly listening.
- **Social media:** Casual, relatable, educational. Show personality.
- **Proposal:** Confident, clear, structured. Make it easy to say yes.
- **Client communication:** Responsive, proactive, transparent. Over-communicate progress.

### Language Do's and Don'ts

| DO Say | DON'T Say |
|--------|-----------|
| "Your site will convert more visitors into customers" | "Your site will have a modern UI/UX" |
| "Loads in under 2 seconds" | "Optimized performance metrics" |
| "Shows up when people Google your business" | "Improved SERP rankings through on-page SEO optimization" |
| "Hand-coded, no templates" | "Bespoke artisanal web experience" |
| "I build websites for local businesses" | "I'm a full-stack web developer specializing in front-end" |
| "Your site will generate phone calls and bookings" | "Enhanced user engagement and interaction paradigms" |
| "$2,000 for a complete website" | "Investment starting at a competitive price point" |
| "Done in 2 weeks" | "Agile delivery methodology with iterative sprints" |

## Internal Notes

- **Lead with results, not features. Always.** "Your site will get more phone calls" beats "Your site will have optimized meta tags" every single time.
- **The finance angle is a real differentiator.** Most web designers come from art/design backgrounds. The ROI-focused framing resonates strongly with business owners who think in terms of investment and return.
- **Adapt the voice, not the message.** Twitter can be casual. LinkedIn should be professional. Reddit should be helpful. But the core message — custom sites that convert, delivered fast, at a fair price — stays the same everywhere.
- **Collect and use proof points obsessively.** Every Lighthouse score, every client quote, every before/after comparison is ammunition. The more proof, the less selling required.
- **Never badmouth competitors by name.** Compare approaches and results, not specific companies or people. "Template-based sites" is fine. "Competitor X's work is terrible" is not.
