## Ideal Client Profiles (Priority Tiers)

### Tier 1: Dental & Healthcare Practices (HIGHEST PRIORITY)
- **Why:** Have the Austin Dental Wellness case study to reference. High-value clients ($2,000-$5,000 deals). Understand professional services. Used to paying for expertise. HIPAA-aware sites are a niche skill.
- **Target titles:** Practice owner, office manager, marketing coordinator
- **Pain points:** Outdated website, bad mobile experience, not showing up in local Google searches, losing patients to competitors with better online presence
- **Search terms for lead gen:** "dentist Austin TX", "dental practice [city]", "orthodontist near me", "pediatric dentist [city]"
- **Pitch angle:** "I helped Austin Dental Wellness go from a 38 to 90+ site speed score while modernizing their entire online presence. I can do the same for your practice."

### Tier 2: Wellness, Fitness & Chiropractic
- **Why:** Adjacent to healthcare, similar pain points, growing industry. Often have terrible websites. Comfortable spending $1,500-$3,000 on business services.
- **Target titles:** Studio owner, practice owner, wellness director
- **Pain points:** Website doesn't reflect the quality of their in-person experience, booking is clunky, not ranking for local searches
- **Search terms:** "yoga studio [city]", "chiropractor [city]", "massage therapy [city]", "personal trainer [city]", "med spa [city]"
- **Pitch angle:** "Your clients judge your business online before they ever walk through the door. Let's make sure your website matches the experience you deliver in person."

### Tier 3: Legal, Accounting & Real Estate
- **Why:** High-value services, understand ROI, willing to invest. Websites are often outdated or template-heavy. Deals tend to be $2,000-$5,000.
- **Target titles:** Managing partner, firm owner, lead agent/broker
- **Pain points:** Website looks like every other firm (generic template), not generating leads, bad mobile experience
- **Search terms:** "law firm [city]", "CPA [city]", "real estate agent [city]", "financial advisor [city]"
- **Pitch angle:** "Your competitors are all using the same WordPress template. A custom-built site that's fast, clean, and optimized for conversions will set you apart immediately."

### Tier 4: Restaurants, Retail & Other Local
- **Why:** High volume of prospects, but lower deal sizes ($500-$1,500). Good for building portfolio and testimonials early on. Move these to Starter tier.
- **Target titles:** Owner, general manager
- **Pain points:** Still relying on Facebook page, website is just a Wix one-pager, menu is a PDF, not showing up on Google Maps
- **Search terms:** "restaurant [city]", "boutique [city]", "salon [city]"
- **Pitch angle:** "You're losing customers to competitors who have better Google presence. I can build you a fast, mobile-friendly site that shows up when people search for [their category] in [their city]."

## Lead Generation Methods

### 1. Google Maps Searches (Primary Method)
- Search by industry + location (e.g., "dentist Austin TX")
- Click through to each business website
- Evaluate: load speed (run quick Lighthouse test), mobile responsiveness, design quality, SEO basics
- If the site is bad: add to outreach list with specific notes on what's wrong
- If no website at all: even better — they need one from scratch

### 2. LeadGen Pro Tool (Automated)
- Use the custom-built Google Maps scraper to pull business listings in bulk
- Filter by: industry, location, whether they have a website
- Cross-reference with Lighthouse API to score sites automatically
- Output: spreadsheet of businesses with bad websites, sorted by opportunity
- Run LeadGen Pro weekly for each target industry in Austin, then expand to other Texas cities

### 3. Yelp Business Listings
- Search by category + location on Yelp
- Click through to business websites linked from Yelp profiles
- Many Yelp-listed businesses have outdated sites or no site at all
- Bonus: Yelp reviews give you intel on the business to personalize outreach

### 4. Technology Detection (Wix/Squarespace/WordPress Identification)
- Use Wappalyzer browser extension to detect what platform a site is built on
- View page source and search for telltale signs:
  - Wix: `wixsite.com` in URL, `<meta name="generator" content="Wix.com">`
  - Squarespace: `squarespace.com` references, `<meta property="og:site_name" content="Squarespace">`
  - WordPress: `/wp-content/`, `/wp-includes/`, `<meta name="generator" content="WordPress">`
- Businesses on these platforms are ideal targets — they already have a site but are limited by the platform
- Pitch: "I noticed your site is on [Wix/Squarespace]. Here's what that's costing you in speed and SEO..."

### 5. Social Media Prospecting
- Search local business hashtags on Twitter/X and Instagram: #AustinBusiness, #AustinTX, #ShopLocal, #[Industry]Austin
- Look at businesses engaging on social but with bad or no websites
- LinkedIn: search for business owners in Austin by title and industry

### 6. Networking & Referrals
- Austin Chamber of Commerce events
- Local BNI or networking groups
- Ask every completed client for 2-3 referrals
- Partner with complementary freelancers (photographers, copywriters, marketing consultants)

## Follow-Up Cadence

| Day | Action | Notes |
|-----|--------|-------|
| Day 1 | Send initial cold email | Use one of the 3 templates above. Always personalize. |
| Day 3 | First follow-up | Short and casual. "Hey [Name], just bumping this up in case it got buried. Happy to send over that free audit whenever you're ready." |
| Day 7 | Final follow-up | Last touch. "Hi [Name], I know you're busy running [Business Name] — totally understand. I'll leave the offer open: if you ever want a free audit of your website, just reply to this email. No pressure, no expiration. Best, Andrew" |
| Day 14+ | Move to nurture list | Add to monthly newsletter list (when created) or revisit in 30-60 days with fresh angle |

**Follow-up rules:**
- Never send more than 3 emails total (initial + 2 follow-ups)
- Never be pushy or salesy in follow-ups. Always keep it helpful and low-pressure.
- If they respond with "not interested," thank them and move on. Never argue.
- If they respond with "not right now," add to 30-day follow-up list.
- If they respond with interest, move immediately to scheduling a discovery call.

## Internal Notes

- **Personalization is non-negotiable.** Every email must reference specific issues with their actual website. Generic emails get ignored.
- **The free audit is the entire strategy.** It's the door-opener, the trust-builder, and the value demonstration all in one. Never skip it.
- **Track everything from day 1.** The data from the first 2 weeks of outreach will determine which templates, industries, and approaches work best. Optimize based on real numbers, not gut feel.
- **Don't chase Tier 4 early.** Focus 80% of outreach on Tiers 1-2 in the first month. Restaurant and retail clients are lower value and higher maintenance.
- **Saturday morning is prime audit time.** Business owners check email on weekends when they're not in meetings. Send audits Friday evening or Saturday morning.
- **Always BCC yourself on every cold email** for records and follow-up tracking.
